# Launch Copy Skill

Write compelling copy for product launches. Landing pages, App Store descriptions, Product Hunt posts, announcement tweets.

## Core Principles

**Lead with the problem, not the product.**
Bad: "Introducing RecipeApp, an AI-powered recipe extraction tool"
Good: "Recipe sites are 90% ads and life stories. Get just the recipe."

**Benefits > Features.**
Feature: "Uses GPT-4 for extraction"
Benefit: "Works on any recipe site in seconds"

**Be specific.**
Vague: "Save time on your workflow"
Specific: "Extract 50 recipes in the time it takes to read one blog post"

**One CTA per surface.**
Landing page: "Start free" or "Get the app" — pick one
Don't: "Sign up, follow us, join Discord, read docs"

## The Formula

### Hook (1 sentence)
State the pain point or transformation. Make them nod.

### Value Prop (1-2 sentences)  
What you do + why it's different. No jargon.

### Proof (optional but powerful)
Numbers, testimonials, logos. If you don't have them, skip — don't fake it.

### CTA (1 clear action)
Tell them exactly what to do next.

## By Surface

### Landing Page Hero
```
[Hook: Pain point as headline]
[Value prop: 1-2 sentence subhead]
[CTA button: Action verb + outcome]
```

Example:
```
Recipe sites are 90% ads.
Get just the recipe — no life stories, no pop-ups, no scrolling.
[Get the App →]
```

### App Store Description
- First line is everything (only ~30 chars visible before "more")
- Lead with what it does, not who you are
- Use short paragraphs (mobile reading)
- Keywords matter but don't stuff

Structure:
```
[One-line value prop]

[2-3 short paragraphs on benefits]

FEATURES:
• Benefit-focused bullet
• Benefit-focused bullet
• Benefit-focused bullet
```

### Product Hunt Tagline
- Max 60 characters
- Formula: [Action] + [Object] + [Differentiator]
- Examples:
  - "Extract recipes without the life stories"
  - "Code review in your PR, not your calendar"

### Product Hunt Description
- First paragraph: Hook + value prop
- Second paragraph: How it works (simple)
- Third paragraph: Who it's for
- Keep under 300 words

### Announcement Tweet
- Hook in first line (make them stop scrolling)
- What it does in second line
- Link or CTA last
- Don't start with "Excited to announce" (everyone does)

Structure:
```
[Hook — pain point or bold claim]

[What you built + key differentiator]

[Link]
```

### Email Announcement
- Subject: Specific > clever (but specific + clever = best)
- First line: Why they should care
- Body: 3-5 short paragraphs max
- One clear CTA — button or link, not both

## Anti-Patterns (Kill These)

From humanizer — these kill credibility:

- ❌ "Revolutionary", "game-changing", "cutting-edge"
- ❌ "Seamlessly integrates", "leverages AI"
- ❌ "Excited to announce", "thrilled to share"
- ❌ "For developers, by developers"
- ❌ "The future of X"
- ❌ Em dash abuse — use commas
- ❌ Rule of three adjectives

## Voice Calibration

**For developer tools:** Direct, slightly irreverent, show you get it
**For consumer apps:** Warm, benefit-focused, minimal jargon
**For B2B:** Professional but not corporate, focus on outcomes

## Process

1. **Identify the surface** — where will this copy live?
2. **Know the audience** — who reads this and what do they care about?
3. **State the pain** — what sucks about the status quo?
4. **Show the transformation** — life after using the product
5. **Write the hook** — stop the scroll
6. **Add proof** — if you have it
7. **One CTA** — what's the next step?
8. **Cut 30%** — first draft is always too long
9. **Read aloud** — if it sounds like marketing, rewrite
